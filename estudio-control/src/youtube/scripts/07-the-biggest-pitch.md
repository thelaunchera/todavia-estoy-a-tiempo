# E07 — THE BIGGEST PITCH
**Series:** OFF BRIEF / FUERA DE BRIEF
**Characters:** NIA, RUBY, ARDEN (fictional retailer marketing lead), FINN (fictional finance lead)
**Setting:** Studio, Northlight Market headquarters
**Target:** 6–8 min with controlled comedic tension
**Status:** Full first-draft dialogue · ready for table read

### SCENE 1 — COLD OPEN: EXECUTIVE MODE
**SHOT:** Nia rehearses in front of a mirror wearing the same cobalt blazer. Ruby sits on a suitcase full of props.

NIA: We are calm. We are prepared. We are professional.
RUBY: You said professional three times. Is that a medical symptom?
NIA: We're pitching Northlight Market in thirty minutes.
RUBY: I know. I wrote it on the ceiling.
**ACTION:** Nia looks up. There is a sticky note on the ceiling: DO NOT PANIC.
NIA: How did you get that up there?
RUBY: Anxiety makes me athletic.
**CUT TO TITLE.**

### SCENE 2 — TWO VISIONS
**SHOT:** Studio whiteboard. Nia's polished deck sits beside Ruby's colorful physical prototype.

NIA: Their brief says they want a launch campaign that can scale nationally.
RUBY: Our idea is a tiny everyday object becoming an unexpected hero.
NIA: Their leadership likes proven, straightforward messaging.
RUBY: You mean leadership likes being bored.
NIA: I mean leadership likes seeing why an idea will work.
RUBY: We can show them both: the logic and the magic.
NIA: We have twelve minutes for the pitch. Six for the concept, four for rollout, two for questions.
RUBY: And zero for a standing ovation?
NIA: The ovation is not in the schedule.
RUBY: Then the schedule lacks vision.

**ACTION:** Nia sees a draft that promises "guaranteed viral results" and deletes it.

NIA: We're not promising numbers we can't guarantee.
RUBY: Agree. We promise a strong idea, solid production, and actual measurement.
NIA: That was a very grown-up sentence.
RUBY: It hurt a little.

### SCENE 3 — THE WAITING ROOM
**SHOT:** Enormous lobby. Nia and Ruby sit beneath a giant corporate mission statement.

RUBY: This lobby has more furniture than our entire net worth.
NIA: Don't calculate that.
RUBY: Too late. The couch is winning.
**ACTION:** A receptionist calls their name. Ruby drops a rubber duck. It slides under the couch.
NIA: Leave it.
RUBY: That's Gregory. He has a family.
NIA: He is plastic.
RUBY: And employed.
**ACTION:** They march toward the conference room. In the last second, Ruby runs back, rescues Gregory, and catches up breathlessly.
NIA: You're thirty years old.
RUBY: And a responsible guardian.

### SCENE 4 — THE PITCH
**SHOT:** Conference table. Arden and Finn watch. The screen shows a strong, simple campaign for everyday home goods.

ARDEN: Tell us what makes your studio different.
NIA: We build campaigns from what a brand actually needs, not from whichever trend happens to be loudest.
RUBY: And we don't think everyday products need to be boring to feel useful.
FINN: What would the production process look like at our scale?
NIA: A pilot, a measurable review, then modular assets adapted to each channel. We have a clear budget and delivery stages.
ARDEN: And the creative idea?
RUBY: We call it "Small Things, Big Days." Ordinary products become tiny heroes in very ordinary disasters.
**ACTION:** Ruby shows a short visual mock-up: a household lamp illuminates a child's lost drawing just in time for a family celebration. Another playful beat features a spatula saving dinner.
ARDEN: That's human. And funny.
FINN: I like the modular approach. But you're a very small team.
NIA: That's true. We won't pretend otherwise. We'd bring in specialists for larger production days and keep our direction accountable.
RUBY: Small team. No mystery layers. The two people pitching are the two people who care.
**ACTION:** Arden smiles.
ARDEN: Refreshingly clear.

### SCENE 5 — THE QUESTION THAT HURTS
**SHOT:** The screen switches to a slide labeled "Growth".

FINN: Suppose we need a fast turnaround with multiple markets. Would you be willing to standardize the creative and reduce experimentation?
NIA: We can standardize deliverables and approval stages.
RUBY: But not eliminate the idea that makes the campaign matter.
NIA: Ruby—
RUBY: Sorry. What I mean is: repetition is useful when it keeps quality consistent. Not when it makes everything feel interchangeable.
FINN: You don't seem to agree.
**ACTION:** Nia catches Ruby's eye, then turns back.
NIA: We agree about the goal. We're discussing how to get there. A pilot would let us test both clarity and distinctiveness before scaling.
ARDEN: That's a reasonable answer.
**ACTION:** Meeting ends. Polite handshakes. They step into an elevator.

### SCENE 6 — THE ELEVATOR FIGHT
**SHOT:** Tight two-shot in reflective elevator. Low mechanical hum.

NIA: You contradicted me in front of their finance lead.
RUBY: I answered a creative question.
NIA: You said they were trying to make everything boring.
RUBY: I specifically did not say boring.
NIA: Your eyebrows did.
RUBY: My eyebrows are independent contractors.
NIA: This could pay our rent for a year.
RUBY: And if we spend that year making things we hate?
NIA: We don't even have an offer yet.
RUBY: Then why are you already giving up?
**ACTION:** The elevator doors open. Neither moves.
ELEVATOR VOICE: Ground floor.
NIA: I am trying to keep us alive.
RUBY: I'm trying to keep us us.
**ACTION:** A stranger clears their throat. They step aside, embarrassed.

### SCENE 7 — THE EMAIL
**SHOT:** Studio at night. They work at opposite desks, not speaking. An email lands.

NIA: They liked the pitch.
RUBY: That's good.
NIA: They want to discuss a paid pilot. There's a budget meeting tomorrow.
RUBY: That's very good.
NIA: And they want us both there.
**ACTION:** Ruby edges toward Nia's desk.

RUBY: Are we okay?
NIA: I don't know. We're tired.
RUBY: I can be less dramatic.
NIA: No, you can't.
RUBY: Accurate.
NIA: I can listen before I defend the spreadsheet.
RUBY: And I can read the spreadsheet before setting it on fire.
**ACTION:** They share a quiet smile.

### SCENE 8 — TAG
**SHOT:** Nia opens the conference folder to prepare. A rubber duck falls out.

NIA: Why is Gregory in the budget proposal?
RUBY: He handles morale.
NIA: Is he billing hours?
RUBY: Not until he gets health insurance.
**ACTION:** Nia replaces the duck on the desk instead of throwing it away.

**END.**
