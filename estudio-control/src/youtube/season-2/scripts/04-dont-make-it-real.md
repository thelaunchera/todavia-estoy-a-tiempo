# SEASON 02 · E04 — DON'T MAKE IT REAL
**Series:** OFF BRIEF — THE CREATIVE HOUSE
**Setting:** Studio interview corner and nearby neighborhood storefront
**Featured cast:** JUNE, SIMONE, NIA, RUBY, VAL, THREE INTERVIEWEES
**Target length:** 6–8 minutes with interview montage, awkward pauses and a sincere ending
**Status:** Full dialogue draft v1 · eight scenes · production not started

### SCENE 1 — COLD OPEN: THE SAME LINE
**SHOT:** Warm cinematic interview close-up. A fictional adult woman faces camera.
INTERVIEWEE 1: This product changed my life.
**CUT:** A sharply different-looking adult man in the same framing.
INTERVIEWEE 2: This product changed my life.
**CUT:** A third adult woman gives exactly the same smile.
INTERVIEWEE 3: This product changed my life.
**ACTION:** Camera pans to June, who lowers the boom mic with increasing disbelief.
JUNE: Are we interviewing people or robots?
SIMONE (O.S.): They're very enthusiastic robots.
JUNE: That's not a defense.
**CUT TO:** OFF BRIEF title with three identical notification pings.

### SCENE 2 — THE BRIEF
**SHOT:** Studio meeting table with samples from Northlight's everyday home line.
NIA: Northlight wants a short testimonial piece about how useful its organizers are in ordinary homes.
SIMONE: Great. I know three people who are perfect on camera.
JUNE: Do they actually use the product?
SIMONE: They are very camera-friendly.
JUNE: That's a different answer.
RUBY: We need real experiences, not actors pretending to love a drawer.
NIA: We also need releases and consent before filming.
VAL: And an interview corner that doesn't look like a dentist's waiting room.
SIMONE: I can get everyone here in an hour.
JUNE: Before that, can I speak with them without a camera?
SIMONE: Wow. A phone call. Vintage.
JUNE: Human beings are a very underrated technology.

### SCENE 3 — THREE PERFECT PEOPLE
**SHOT:** A cheerful interview set. Nia monitors. Simone waits just out of frame with cue cards.
INTERVIEWEE 1: It changed my life.
JUNE: That's quite a claim. Can you tell me one thing you actually use it for?
INTERVIEWEE 1: I... haven't taken mine out of the box.
**ACTION:** June stops herself from reacting.
JUNE: Thank you for telling me. That's helpful.
**CUT:** Next interview.
INTERVIEWEE 2: It's so life-changing.
JUNE: What's inside yours right now?
INTERVIEWEE 2: A plant.
VAL: Isn't it a desk organizer?
INTERVIEWEE 2: It's a very organized plant.
**CUT:** Third interview.
INTERVIEWEE 3: This product changed my life.
JUNE: Did someone ask you to say that?
INTERVIEWEE 3: Simone gave me a very helpful note.
**ACTION:** All eyes shift to Simone.
SIMONE: It was a suggestion.
JUNE: Printed in twelve-point bold.

### SCENE 4 — THE UNCOMFORTABLE DISCUSSION
**SHOT:** Crew in the hall. June holds her notebook; Simone has a tablet of planned talking points.
SIMONE: They're here. They look great. Why can't we use the lines?
JUNE: Because we don't know if the words are true.
SIMONE: The product is good.
JUNE: Then real details should be enough.
NIA: We cannot present prepared claims as independent customer experiences.
SIMONE: Okay. You're right. I wanted to make sure the shoot didn't fall flat.
RUBY: Sometimes the perfect line is the least interesting one.
SIMONE: So what do we do? Send everyone home?
JUNE: Not necessarily. We thank them, explain the change, and ask whether they have any genuine experience they want to share. If not, no testimonial.
VAL: I can shoot a real-use demonstration without pretending it's a personal story.
NIA: We confirm the revised concept with the client first.
SIMONE: Fine. I'll call them. But I am officially retiring the phrase "changed my life."
JUNE: You lasted longer than I expected.

### SCENE 5 — THE UNEXPECTED STORY
**SHOT:** A neighborhood bookstore and its tiny office. June speaks to the owner as Val frames unobtrusive documentary coverage.
BOOKSTORE OWNER: I use the desk organizer because my receipts used to disappear into the mystery drawer.
JUNE: What's the mystery drawer?
BOOKSTORE OWNER: Pens, keys, old batteries, a birthday card from 2017. A key to a lock I don't own.
VAL: That is an incredible drawer.
JUNE: Do you still have it?
BOOKSTORE OWNER: Of course. It's where I keep my bad decisions.
**ACTION:** All laugh genuinely.
BOOKSTORE OWNER: This thing just helps me find the order forms before customers walk in.
JUNE: That's a real reason. Can we film you showing us?
BOOKSTORE OWNER: Sure, but the drawer has to approve its close-up.
VAL: I'll sign a separate release for the drawer.
**ACTION:** They set up a gentle demonstration, with no exaggerated result.

### SCENE 6 — SMALL TRUTH, BIGGER FEELING
**SHOT:** Back in the studio. Nova's rough cut (visible on the monitor) shows a personal, warmly funny moment.
SIMONE: The bookstore owner is charming.
JUNE: She was honest. I didn't have to coach her.
RUBY: Her mystery drawer deserves a spin-off.
NIA: The client wants emotional, but this is specific. We can support every claim.
VAL: And she looks good because she forgot the camera was there.
SIMONE: I spent all morning looking for camera-ready talent when I needed someone with a real drawer.
JUNE: You found us the owner. That's valuable.
SIMONE: Wait, that was a compliment?
JUNE: Don't get used to it.
NIA: Let's send the revised cut for approval.

### SCENE 7 — CLIENT FEEDBACK
**SHOT:** Team video call with fictional Northlight marketing lead, Arden.
ARDEN (VIDEO): This feels different. In a good way.
NIA: We changed the direction from scripted praise to an actual use case, and we have permission to use it.
ARDEN: I love the details. That drawer is everybody's drawer.
RUBY: It has its own audience now.
ARDEN: Approved. Can we get a cutdown centered on the mystery drawer?
VAL: Absolutely. We already have the footage.
JUNE: And we have a release for the owner. The drawer declined legal representation.
**ACTION:** Arden laughs. Simone fist-bumps June, who is surprised but smiles.

### SCENE 8 — TAG: A REAL TESTIMONIAL
**SHOT:** Quiet studio. Simone looks into an unused camera.
SIMONE: Since we're doing honesty, here's my actual testimonial.
JUNE (O.S.): About what?
SIMONE: Working here has changed my life.
JUNE (O.S.): Did it?
SIMONE: I now know what a release form is, I fear bold print, and I have met a very interesting drawer.
JUNE: That is surprisingly specific.
SIMONE: I learn.
**END — CUT TO CREDITS.**
