# SEASON 02 · E02 — TEN DIRECTORS, ONE SHOT
**Series:** OFF BRIEF — THE CREATIVE HOUSE
**Setting:** Studio set, adjacent prop area
**Featured cast:** RUBY, VAL, SAGE, MATEO, NIA (plus one off-screen crew voice)
**Target length:** 6–8 minutes with escalating repeated takes and silent reaction beats
**Status:** Full dialogue draft v1 · eight scenes · production not started

### SCENE 1 — COLD OPEN: THE CUP
**SHOT:** Immaculate close-up: one steaming coffee cup on a smooth warm-ivory tabletop. Nothing moves.
VAL (O.S.): Nobody. Touch. The cup.
SAGE (O.S.): It needs to be three centimeters left.
MATEO (O.S.): The cup is not the story.
RUBY (O.S.): Okay, hear me out.
**ACTION:** A hand darts into shot and rotates the cup. Every voice stops.
VAL (O.S.): WHO TOUCHED THE CUP?
**SHOT:** The camera pulls back to reveal a cramped semicircle of crew members pointing in different directions. Ruby slowly puts her hands behind her back.
RUBY: In my defense, it looked emotionally unavailable.
**CUT:** OFF BRIEF title, accompanied by a tiny cup clink.

### SCENE 2 — THE SIMPLE ASSIGNMENT
**SHOT:** Nia presents a one-page creative brief to Ruby, Val, Sage and Mateo.
NIA: Northlight wants one clean opening shot for its everyday-home campaign.
VAL: Product centered, beautiful light, no movement. That's ten minutes.
SAGE: Centered in what? Cream? Oat? Warm oat? Those are different.
MATEO: Copy line: "Small moments, better days."
RUBY: It's nice. But where's the surprise?
NIA: The surprise is that the client receives a finished shot.
RUBY: Hurtful.
VAL: I'll set the camera. Sage controls the surface. Ruby approves the performance. Mateo approves the copy later.
MATEO: I would like to approve silence.
RUBY: Welcome to my favorite kind of teamwork.

### SCENE 3 — FIRST TAKE
**SHOT:** Val carefully adjusts focus. Sage fusses with a folded linen napkin. Ruby watches monitor.
VAL: Rolling. Locked frame. Hold.
RUBY: Wait. Maybe a hand enters with the cup.
VAL: Then it's not a locked product frame.
SAGE: If a hand enters, that sleeve needs to be ivory, not gray.
MATEO: What does the hand want?
NIA: A cup of coffee?
MATEO: Finally. A character with motivation.
RUBY: Okay, a hand enters like the coffee has just saved somebody's morning.
VAL: And whose hand is it?
**ACTION:** Everyone looks at Nia.
NIA: Absolutely not. I am client management.
RUBY: Exactly. A natural actor.
**ACTION:** Nia reluctantly reaches into frame. Sage stops her.
SAGE: Your sleeve is blue.
NIA: My entire life is blue.

### SCENE 4 — TOO MANY ADJUSTMENTS
**SHOT:** Short rhythmic montage. An identical slate claps again and again; the setup changes a little each time.
VAL: Take four. Do not move the light.
SAGE: I only moved the reflector.
VAL: That's the light.
SAGE: That's the *quality* of the light.
MATEO: Maybe the line should be "A little better, every day."
RUBY: That's cute.
NIA: That's an entirely different script.
MATEO: It's eight words shorter than our argument.
VAL: Camera ready.
RUBY: What if the cup spins?
VAL: Then the coffee spins.
SAGE: Then the napkin clashes.
NIA: Then the schedule dies.
**ACTION:** Everyone speaks at once. The coffee mug remains perfectly still as if it has quit.
MATEO: I think the cup is directing this production now.

### SCENE 5 — RUBY LOSES THE ROOM
**SHOT:** Ruby climbs onto the little step used for overhead angles. Everyone freezes, listening.
RUBY: Okay, stop! I need you all to trust the vision.
VAL: Which vision? We've had six.
SAGE: Eight if you count the napkins.
MATEO: Nine if you count the one where the coffee had feelings.
RUBY: I said *emotional stakes*. Not *coffee therapy*.
NIA: Ruby, you're the director. Direct.
RUBY: I AM.
NIA: You're proposing new ideas. Directing means choosing one.
**ACTION:** Ruby feels that. She looks at the shot list.
RUBY: You're right. I kept asking for more because I was scared my first idea wasn't special.
VAL: Your first idea was good.
SAGE: It needed a better background.
MATEO: And fewer words.
RUBY: That's the nicest insult I've ever received.

### SCENE 6 — ONE CLEAR DIRECTION
**SHOT:** Ruby places tape marks on the set.
RUBY: Here's the shot. Plain table. Cup arrives. A tiny everyday disaster is heard off-screen. The hand pauses, then lifts the cup like a victory.
VAL: One camera move?
RUBY: Slow push-in. Only on the lift.
SAGE: Linen and a warm neutral. No props competing.
MATEO: One line at the end. Not in the image.
NIA: Time?
VAL: Seven minutes to reset, two takes.
NIA: I love a sentence with a number.
RUBY: Then we're actually a team.
MATEO: That sounds suspiciously like the campaign tagline.

### SCENE 7 — THE TAKE THAT WORKS
**SHOT:** In-camera final: coffee arrives; offscreen a stack of papers drops; the hand freezes and takes a triumphant sip.
VAL: Cut.
**ACTION:** Everyone watches playback in silence.
SAGE: The warm tone is beautiful.
MATEO: The pause is funny.
NIA: And we hit the schedule.
RUBY: I know. It's disturbing.
VAL: Should we do a safety?
RUBY: One safety, same setup. No new concepts.
**ACTION:** Everyone stares at her.
RUBY: What? I contain multitudes and a shot list.
NIA: Put that on your business card.

### SCENE 8 — TAG: THE TINIEST NOTE
**SHOT:** End of day. The monitor shows the final composed product frame.
NIA: The client said it's perfect.
RUBY: Finally, a complete sentence.
NIA: One tiny note.
**ACTION:** Five people stiffen at once.
VAL: No.
SAGE: Absolutely not.
MATEO: I resign from the sentence.
NIA: They want to know if the cup comes in other colors.
**ACTION:** Silence. Ruby cracks a smile.
RUBY: Great. Because Sage has twenty-three options.
SAGE: Twenty-four. I've been working.
**END — CUT TO CREDITS.**
